DDD is hard to understand, so hard to implement.

let's keep some simple rules:

Value Objects are important, and should be immutable. They hold knowledge about the domain, and enforce invariants on
the data they represent.

Entities are objects that have identity. They hold knowledge about the domain, and enforce invariants on the data they
represent.

Aggregates are groups of entities and value objects that are treated as a single unit.But some people say "kill the
aggregates".

Invariants are enforced at their consistency boundary: on the VO or Entity when they're local, on the aggregate root
when they span multiple entities within the same aggregate.

Business rules are behaviors: they live inside the aggregate that owns the relevant data by default. Only delegate a
rule to a Domain Service when it doesn't legitimately belong to any single aggregate, or when it requires collaboration
across multiple aggregates.

Key distinction to preserve:

Invariant — a property that must always hold, checked on every state transition. A static consistency constraint.
Business rule — a decision or behavior: what is permitted, computed, or triggered in a given situation. Transition
logic, not a permanent constraint.

The default location for a business rule is the aggregate, not a service. A Domain Service is the justified exception,
not the starting point. And coordination that involves loading, deciding, and persisting across aggregates is an
Application Service concern, not a Domain Service one — keep those two layers distinct.
